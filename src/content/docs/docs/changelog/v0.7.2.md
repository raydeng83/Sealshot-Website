**Released July 22, 2026** ·
[Download](https://github.com/raydeng83/Sealshot-Release/releases/tag/v0.7.2)

The current release. Recovery gets a real safety net — including a way back
into the app when a recovery code is lost — plus whole-library export and a
14-day free trial.

## What's new since 0.7.1

### Security & recovery

- **Save or print your Recovery Kit** — the recovery-key screen now has
  **Save Kit…** and **Print…**, so a copy of your code with instructions can
  live on paper or in your own backup, not just on the clipboard. See
  [Save your recovery code](/docs/guide/security/#save-your-recovery-code).
- **Periodic recovery-code checks** — about every three months Sealshot asks
  you to confirm you still have your code. Never while the library is locked
  or more than once per launch, and you can always defer or generate a fresh
  code on the spot.
- **A way forward if you lose everything** — the lock screen now offers
  **"I can't unlock…"** for when both Touch ID and your recovery code are
  gone. It explains why Sealshot can't bypass encryption, then (after you
  type RESET) moves the encrypted captures to a read-only **Locked Archive**
  and turns encryption off so you can keep working. **Nothing is deleted.**
- **Locked Archive is visible and restorable** — archived captures appear as
  their own Library section, dimmed with a lock badge. Find your code later
  and **Restore…** brings them back, re-enabling encryption with the
  recovered key. Items set aside by turning encryption off (rather than by a
  reset) can't be restored with a code, and Sealshot now says so plainly
  instead of implying otherwise.
- Entering your recovery code — to restore an archive or unlock on a new Mac
  — counts as verifying it, so you aren't asked again sooner than necessary.

### Library

- **Export All Files…** — right-click **All Files** in the sidebar to bundle
  your whole library into one package you can AirDrop to another Mac and
  import, optionally passphrase-protected. Works like exporting a
  collection, just for everything. See
  [Sharing & export](/docs/guide/sharing/).
- Package export shows how many items are done ("Packaging 5 of 10…")
  instead of a time-left estimate.

### Licensing

- **Sealshot now has a 14-day free trial.** After it, your existing captures
  stay fully viewable, editable, and exportable — a license is needed only
  for *new* captures and recordings. Activate by opening your license file
  in **Settings → License**; a banner appears in the trial's final week.
- Installing a version released after your license's update window shows a
  renewal banner and pauses new captures until you renew or return to a
  covered version. Everything already captured stays fully usable.

### Fixes

- Dragging a single capture from the recent strip to Finder now drops a
  real, full-resolution image file instead of a broken alias.
- The empty area around the image in the editor behaves like canvas:
  clicking it deselects, and you can start a selection box out there and
  drag across the image.
- A selection box now selects only the objects it **fully encloses**,
  instead of everything it touches on the way past.
- The Editor/Library/Settings switcher no longer offers an Icon/Text
  right-click menu that could leave a stray "View" label behind.
  (macOS 15 and later.)

### Other

- **Live Captures get real metadata** — keywords and a summary listing each
  captured window with a one-line description. Scenes taken before this
  update pick theirs up next time you open them.
- Sealshot now says when capture summaries need Apple Intelligence turned
  on — and says plainly when your Mac can't run them — instead of quietly
  hiding them. Turning Apple Intelligence on while a capture is open resumes
  its summary automatically.
- The Library and Settings tabs use a solid backdrop instead of the
  hex-pattern background.
